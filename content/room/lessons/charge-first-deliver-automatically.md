# Charge First, Deliver Automatically

*Another real chapter from TransitNow Logistics Services — the founder of this Room built this into her business in September 2026. Same rule as always: this is what actually happened, not theory.*

## The Problem Nobody Wants to Talk About

For a while, TransitNow's dispatch service had a leak: work could start before payment was confirmed, and when a card failed, somebody had to chase it by hand. Every business owner knows this feeling — you're delivering the service and the money is somewhere behind you, maybe coming, maybe not.

That is backwards. You do not get paid for work you already did on hope.

## The Fix: Stripe Is the Source of Truth

Now the money system runs the delivery system — not the other way around. Here is how it works:

- When someone subscribes (Basic $50/month or Complete $100/month), Stripe records it and the driver is tagged as paid — automatically.
- Every monthly renewal keeps them active. No renewal, no active tag.
- A failed card flips them to past-due and triggers an automatic "update your card" email. Nobody chases anything by hand.
- A cancellation flips them to canceled, and dispatch work stops.

And the rule that holds it all together: **no actual dispatch work starts or gets assigned without an active paid subscription.** The application is still free — anyone can apply. But the service is gated. That boundary protects the business.

**The takeaway:** Build the money system BEFORE the delivery system. Payment is not a detail you bolt on later — it is the gate that makes the whole thing real.

## Tiers That Mean Something

Basic and Complete used to be just two prices. Now they are two different service levels, enforced by the system itself:

- **Basic — $50/month. "Start steady."** Full onboarding, then 2 route matches every Monday from real opportunities in your city and state. Your pipeline builds week by week.
- **Complete — $100/month. "Grow faster."** Everything in Basic, plus 5 route matches on day one — a full board from the start — refilled back to 5 every Monday, and Complete drivers get matched first, ahead of Basic.

The system reads the plan tag from Stripe and delivers the right tier. Upgrade from Basic to Complete and your matches step up the same week. Payment fails and matches pause until it is fixed. No spreadsheets, no memory, no favors.

**The takeaway:** A tier is not a price — it is a promise of a specific level of service. Write down exactly what each tier gets, then make the system deliver it without you touching it.

## Automation That Sells the Value

Every Monday, the system matches every paid driver to the most recent real opportunities in their city and state, and sends them by email and text. Drivers also set their own weekly goal — most aim for $750 to $1,000 — and watch their assigned routes stack up against it.

And here is the part that matters: if there are no real opportunities in a driver's city, the system says so honestly. It never invents loads. Saying "nothing real right now" costs nothing and protects everything — one fake match destroys trust forever.

**The takeaway:** Automate the delivery of your value, and let the automation itself prove the value. When the system does what you said it would do, every single week, you stop selling and start keeping.

## The Honest-Marketing Rule

Here is the line we do not cross: the tiers sell the **growth difference** — 2 matches vs 5, steady vs faster, matched first vs matched after. We never print what a driver "could make" on either tier. No estimates, no ranges, no "up to."

Why? Because the moment you print an income figure next to your price, it becomes an earnings claim — and one earnings claim wipes out every no-guarantee protection you built. The whole business is worded carefully so it never promises routes, loads, contracts, or earnings. One sentence of hype would undo all of it.

**The takeaway:** Sell the service difference in hard numbers. Let the customer imagine the upside. Your job is to describe what you deliver — exactly — and let the results speak.

## The Core Lesson

Most people build the product first and figure out payment later. That is how you end up doing free work and chasing money. Do it the other way: charge first, then deliver automatically. The money system is not the boring part of the business — it is the part that makes the business real.
